Underflap Glider Paper Airplane

The extra underside flaps give the wings rigidity and help it glide better. It is a intermediate fold for long floating glides, shown in 6 steps (folds with hands only).

In calm air with a level throw, expect about 10–14 m (33–46 ft), ranking #23 of 37 for distance. Treat the numbers as a trim target: if your fold lands short, check symmetry and crease sharpness first.

How to throw
Launch softly at a slight upward angle, ideally from a high point, and let the plane glide instead of forcing it.

Folding steps

1Step 1

Crease the sheet both ways, then open it flat.

2Step 2

With the sheet landscape, fold the top edge down to the centre crease.

3Step 3

Now fold the top edge down on the centre crease.

4Step 4

Fold the top-right corner over down to the centre fold as shown.

5Step 5

Do the same fold on the left.

6Step 6

Fold that corner down again to meet the horizontal crease.

7Step 7

Repeat step six on the other side as shown in the guide.

8Step 8

Next, fold the top point down to meet the horizontal fold as shown in the guide.

9Step 9

Crease the sheet plane in half away from you.

10Step 10

Fold one wing out, leaving about 1 inch (2.5cm) for the body of the airplane as shown in the guide.

11Step 11

Finally, fold down the second wing to match as shown in the guide.

Frequently asked questions

How far can the Underflap Glider fly?

With a calm day and a level throw, expect roughly 10–14 metres (about 33–46 feet). Among the 37 distance-rated designs in this collection it ranks #23. Wind, paper weight, and small crease errors all change the result.

How long does the Underflap Glider stay in the air?

A well-trimmed Underflap Glider should hold a very brief flight; the design is not aimed at hang time. Launching from a higher spot and keeping the wings level will extend it.

What is the Underflap Glider best for?

It is best for long floating glides. Folding skill is intermediate. Use Letter & A4, 11 steps, hands only.

How should I throw the Underflap Glider?

Launch softly at a slight upward angle, ideally from a high point, and let the plane glide instead of forcing it.
