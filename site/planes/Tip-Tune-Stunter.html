Tip-Tune Stunter Paper Airplane

Adjust the wingtips to make it spin or flip. It is a advanced fold for tricks and loops, shown in 8 steps (folds with hands only).

The Tip-Tune Stunter is built more for shape and handling than for raw range or hang time. See the measured results table for how we score designs that do fly for distance or hang time.

Folding steps

1Step 1

Crease both corners down and open them to leave an X.

2Step 2

Fold the top so the new crease crosses the centre of that X.

3Step 3

Bring both top corners in so they meet the midline.

4Step 4

Undo the last fold, then accordion-tuck the sides inside.

5Step 5

Fold in the bottom layer's sides — the crease should run through the bottom corners.

6Step 6

Fold the large triangle in half away from you, then fold it back so a little tip sticks up.

7Step 7

Fold the plane in half away you as shown in the guide.

8Step 8

Finally, fold the sides out to create the wings. The body should be about one inch (2cm) tall as shown in the guide.

Frequently asked questions

How far can the Tip-Tune Stunter fly?

The Tip-Tune Stunter is not scored for distance in this hangar — it is not built for range. See the measured results table above the folding steps for metres, hangar rank, and the fixed protocol (70–80 gsm paper, ~1.6 m shoulder-height release, calm air, best of 10 throws per person).

How long does the Tip-Tune Stunter stay in the air?

The Tip-Tune Stunter is not scored for hang time here — the design is not aimed at time aloft. See the measured results table above the folding steps for metres, hangar rank, and the fixed protocol (70–80 gsm paper, ~1.6 m shoulder-height release, calm air, best of 10 throws per person).

What is the Tip-Tune Stunter best for?

It is best for tricks and loops. Folding skill is advanced. Use Letter & A4, 8 steps, hands only.

How should I throw the Tip-Tune Stunter?

Use a quick wrist snap and a fairly firm throw so the plane has enough speed to complete its maneuver.
