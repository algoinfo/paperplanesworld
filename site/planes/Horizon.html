Horizon Paper Airplane

This reliable design likes to stay up in the air and fly for a long long ways. It is a master fold for far, straight throws, shown in 6 steps (folds with hands only).

In calm air with a level throw, expect about 13–18 m (43–59 ft), ranking #16 of 37 for distance; roughly 8–10 s of hang time (#3 of 36). Treat the numbers as a trim target: if your fold lands short, check symmetry and crease sharpness first.

How to throw
Hold the plane level and throw with a smooth, firm push, aiming straight ahead rather than upward.

Estimates assume a calm day, a level launch, and a well-trimmed plane.

Folding steps

1Step 1

Make a vertical centre crease, open it, then bring both top corners into the centre.

2Step 2

Bring the tip to the bottom edge — on A4, stop about 2 cm short of the edge.

3Step 3

Fold the tip back up level with the triangle flaps, with about 3 cm of overlap.

4Step 4

Fold the upper right into the centre, then pull the lower right out sideways and flatten; mirror on the left.

5Step 5

Reach in, pull the centre flaps out, refold them the opposite way, then fold the sides in.

6Step 6

Fold each centre flap's bottom edge back at a slight angle, tuck it under, and press flat.

Frequently asked questions

How far can the Horizon fly?

With a calm day and a level throw, expect roughly 13–18 metres (about 43–59 feet). Among the 37 distance-rated designs in this collection it ranks #16. Wind, paper weight, and small crease errors all change the result.

How long does the Horizon stay in the air?

A well-trimmed Horizon should hold about 8–10 seconds of hang time. That places it #3 of 36 designs for time aloft. Launching from a higher spot and keeping the wings level will extend it.

What is the Horizon best for?

It is best for far, straight throws. Folding skill is master. Use Letter & A4, 6 steps, hands only.

How should I throw the Horizon?

Hold the plane level and throw with a smooth, firm push, aiming straight ahead rather than upward.
