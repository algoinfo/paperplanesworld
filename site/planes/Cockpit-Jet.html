Cockpit Jet Paper Airplane

This design looks like a fighter jet, complete with a cockpit for the pilot to fly the paper airplane. It is a advanced fold for long floating glides, shown in 10 steps (folds with hands only).

Metres, hang-time seconds, and hangar ranks are in the measured results table below — before the folding steps — with the fixed test protocol.

Flight notes

Best for
Long floats — long floating glides
Folding skill
Advanced
Paper & folds
Letter & A4 · 10 folds · Hands only
How to throw
Launch gently at a slight upward angle, ideally from a high spot, and let it settle into a glide instead of throwing it hard.

Metres, hang time, and hangar ranks are in the measured results table before the folding steps.

Measured results

Hangar figures for this design — distance in metres, hang time in seconds, and rank among scored planes — before you fold.

Metric Measured result Hangar rank
Distance 19–26 m (62–85 ft) #3 of 37
Hang time 4–5 s #26 of 36

Test conditions (same for every design we score): 70–80 gsm Letter or A4 printer paper; standing release at about 1.6 m shoulder height; calm indoor air (no wind); each thrower gets 10 attempts and we keep their best throw. Ranks compare only designs that cleared the scoring threshold under this protocol.

Folding steps

1Step 1

Close the plane with a vertical fold.

2Step 2

Open it, then crease it horizontally in half.

3Step 3

Fully open the sheet, then bring both top corners into the centre.

4Step 4

Fold both top edges into the centre once more.

5Step 5

Turn the plane over and fold the tip down to the bottom edge.

6Step 6

Flip it again and bring both corners into the centre.

7Step 7

Again, flip it around and fold the point back up as shown in the guide.

8Step 8

Flip it around one last time and fold the center flaps out as in the photo.

9Step 9

Next, fold the plane in half towards you as shown in the guide.

10Step 10

Finally, fold out both wings as shown in the guide.

Frequently asked questions

How far can the Cockpit Jet fly?

Our measured hangar result is 19–26 metres (about 62–85 feet), ranking #3 of 37 distance-rated designs. See the measured results table above the folding steps for metres, hangar rank, and the fixed protocol (70–80 gsm paper, ~1.6 m shoulder-height release, calm air, best of 10 throws per person).

How long does the Cockpit Jet stay in the air?

Our measured hang time is 4–5 seconds, #26 of 36 for time aloft. See the measured results table above the folding steps for metres, hangar rank, and the fixed protocol (70–80 gsm paper, ~1.6 m shoulder-height release, calm air, best of 10 throws per person).

What is the Cockpit Jet best for?

It is best for long floating glides. Folding skill is advanced. Use Letter & A4, 10 steps, hands only.

How should I throw the Cockpit Jet?

Launch gently at a slight upward angle, ideally from a high spot, and let it settle into a glide instead of throwing it hard.
