Blunt-Nose Cruiser Paper Airplane

This paper airplane design has a thick blunt nose that makes it very durable. It is a intermediate fold for long floating glides, shown in 6 steps (folds with hands only).

In calm air with a level throw, expect about 16–21 m (52–69 ft), ranking #8 of 37 for distance; roughly 4–5 s of hang time (#24 of 36). Treat the numbers as a trim target: if your fold lands short, check symmetry and crease sharpness first.

How to throw
Launch gently at a slight upward angle, ideally from a high spot, and let it settle into a glide instead of throwing it hard.

Estimates assume a calm day, a level launch, and a well-trimmed plane.

Frequently asked questions

How far can the Blunt-Nose Cruiser fly?

With a calm day and a level throw, expect roughly 16–21 metres (about 52–69 feet). Among the 37 distance-rated designs in this collection it ranks #8. Wind, paper weight, and small crease errors all change the result.

How long does the Blunt-Nose Cruiser stay in the air?

A well-trimmed Blunt-Nose Cruiser should hold about 4–5 seconds of hang time. That places it #24 of 36 designs for time aloft. Launching from a higher spot and keeping the wings level will extend it.

What is the Blunt-Nose Cruiser best for?

It is best for long floating glides. Folding skill is intermediate. Use Letter & A4, 13 steps, hands only.

How should I throw the Blunt-Nose Cruiser?

Launch gently at a slight upward angle, ideally from a high spot, and let it settle into a glide instead of throwing it hard.
