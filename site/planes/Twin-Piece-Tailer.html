Twin-Piece Tailer Paper Airplane

A unique design that uses one piece of paper, but cuts it into two pieces that you put back together to make one paper airplane. It is a intermediate fold for long floating glides, shown in 6 steps (needs a small cut).

The Twin-Piece Tailer is built more for shape and handling than for raw range or hang time.

How to throw
Launch gently at a slight upward angle, ideally from a high spot, and let it settle into a glide instead of throwing it hard.

Estimates assume a calm day, a level launch, and a well-trimmed plane.

Folding steps

1Step 1

Crease diagonally as shown, trim the leftover strip into a square, and keep the offcut for later.

2Step 2

Fold the left corner to the opposite corner and open it to leave an X crease.

3Step 3

Next accordion-tuck the sides in to form a triangle.

4Step 4

Bring both bottom corners up to the top point.

5Step 5

Take the earlier offcut and fold its top corners into the centre to make an arrow.

6Step 6

Slide the arrow fully into the plane's body.

7Step 7

Next, fold the tip of the airplane down to secure the inner arrow as shown in the guide.

8Step 8

Finally, tuck both center flaps into the top piece and then slightly crease the plane in half as shown in the guide.

Frequently asked questions

How far can the Twin-Piece Tailer fly?

With a calm day and a level throw, expect only a short distance, since this design is not built for range. Wind, paper weight, and small crease errors all change the result.

How long does the Twin-Piece Tailer stay in the air?

A well-trimmed Twin-Piece Tailer should hold a very brief flight; the design is not aimed at hang time. Launching from a higher spot and keeping the wings level will extend it.

What is the Twin-Piece Tailer best for?

It is best for long floating glides. Folding skill is intermediate. Use Letter & A4, 8 steps, needs a cut.

How should I throw the Twin-Piece Tailer?

Launch gently at a slight upward angle, ideally from a high spot, and let it settle into a glide instead of throwing it hard.
