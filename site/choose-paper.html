Choosing paper for a paper airplane

Printer paper is the right place to start. gsm, A4 versus Letter, and fair comparisons between sheets.

Paper Planes World · 3 min read

In short: Standard 70–80 gsm A4 or US Letter printer paper works for every design here. Skip card stock. Compare two papers only after folding the same plane the same way.

What gsm means

gsm is grams per square metre. Around 70–80 gsm folds cleanly and still holds a crease outdoors. Lighter sheets glide softly indoors; heavier sheets keep sharp folds but dive sooner if the design is already nose-heavy.

A4 or Letter

Both work. A4 is slightly narrower and taller than US Letter, which shifts wing proportions by a few millimetres. Fold to the sheet’s own edges and compare estimated distances instead of forcing a resize.

What to avoid

Card stock and construction paper make dense planes that stall almost immediately. Wrinkled reused sheets hide symmetry errors. For a fair paper test, fold two identical planes and alternate throws — see the fair flight test guide.

Quick answers

What paper should I use for paper airplanes?

Use ordinary A4 or US Letter printer paper around 70–80 gsm. It folds easily, holds creases, and matches the estimates on every guide page on this site.

Can I use notebook paper?

Yes for practice, but lined notebook sheets are often lighter and smaller. Expect shorter range than the site estimates, and do not mix notebook and printer results in one test.
