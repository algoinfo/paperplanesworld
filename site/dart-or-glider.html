Dart or glider: which paper airplane should you make?

Pick a plane by what you want it to do and where you will throw it. Darts punch forward; gliders trade speed for time aloft.

AirePlanes · 3 min read

In short: Want distance in a hallway? Start with a dart such as Classic Dart or Rocket-Launch Dart. Want slow indoor float? Pick a glider such as Silent High Glider. Want tricks? Choose an acrobatic design and trim the trailing edges up.

Darts: speed and distance

Darts have narrow noses and swept wings that cut drag. They like a firm, level throw and reward sharp centre creases. On this site, Classic Dart is the easiest entry; Rocket-Launch Dart and Zip Dart push farther once your folds are clean.

Gliders: hang time

Gliders spread more wing area and fly slower. They need a gentle release and careful trailing-edge trim — too much upward bend stalls them. Silent High Glider and Sea Glider are strong hang-time picks for calm indoor air.

Stunts and hybrids

Acrobatic designs such as Loop Plane and Boomerang use trim and launch angle for loops or turns. Hybrids that list both distance and time aloft sit between dart and glider — useful when you want one plane for mixed games.

Best beginner paper airplane

If you want one first fold, use Classic Dart: Easy difficulty, no scissors, clear steps. Kids and classrooms should stay on Easy designs until symmetry becomes automatic, then move to Medium gliders.

Quick answers

What is the easiest paper airplane to make?

Classic Dart is the easiest starter in this collection: straight folds, Easy difficulty, and no scissors. It also flies far enough to feel rewarding on the first try.

Dart or glider — which flies farther?

Darts usually win pure forward distance with a firm level throw. Gliders travel less far but stay up longer. Pick the metric that matches your game.
