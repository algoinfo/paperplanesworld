How to throw a paper airplane

A smooth forward push with level wings beats a hard baseball-style throw. Match the angle and force to the design: darts like speed, gliders like softness.

Paper Planes World · 3 min read

In short: Hold under the wings, keep them level, push straight forward from the shoulder, and release with the nose near level. Start gently; add speed only if the paper stays stiff.

Throw like a dart, not a ball

Beginners often throw paper airplanes like baseballs — a big arc and maximum force. Timing errors then tip the nose or bank a wing. Instead, hold the plane near your shoulder and extend the arm straight forward, releasing at the end of a smooth push.

Pick an angle for the job

Most distance darts fly farthest with a firm, nearly level release. Many gliders prefer a gentle toss with the nose slightly up. Steep upward throws waste speed and invite stalls unless you are practising loops with a stunt design such as Loop Plane.

Force: narrow planes vs wide wings

Narrow darts can take a harder throw. Wide wings flex and flutter if you overspeed them, so start soft and increase only while the flight stays smooth. If every throw looks different, reduce force until three flights match — then diagnose the plane.

How to make it fly farther

Symmetry and a sharp centre crease matter more than raw power. Launch level, keep both trailing edges matched, and change only one trim at a time. Compare against the distance estimate on each plane’s guide page.

Quick answers

What is the best angle to throw a paper airplane?

For most distance designs, release nearly level or a few degrees nose-up. Gliders often want a slightly higher, softer toss. Only stunt loops need a steep upward throw.

Should I throw a paper airplane as hard as I can?

No. Excess speed flexes wide wings and hides trim problems. Push smoothly, then add force only while three consecutive flights stay consistent.
